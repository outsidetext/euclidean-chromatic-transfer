\begin{abstract}
Let $\chid{D}$ denote the minimum number of colors required to color the
plane so that no two points whose distance lies in $D$ receive the same
color, and set $\dd=(\sqrt6+\sqrt2)/2=2\cos(\pi/12)$, so that
$\dd^2=2+\sqrt3$ and $\dd^2-1=\sqrt2\,\dd=1+\sqrt3$.  Our main result is
\[
  \chid{\{1,\dd\}}\;\ge\;7
\]
for arbitrary colorings, with no measurability assumed (the averaging uses an invariant mean on the amenable group $E(2)$, a theorem of ZFC): for every proper
$6$-coloring, invariant monochromatic-pair densities $p_{\sqrt2}$ and
$p_{1+\sqrt3}$ would have to satisfy $p_{\sqrt2}+p_{1+\sqrt3}\ge 1$
(an $8$-point pigeonhole gadget of Parts) and $p_{\sqrt2},p_{1+\sqrt3}\le
5/11$ (two explicit $12$-point chain configurations whose long-range pairs
land exactly on the forbidden distances via the identity
$\dd^2-1=\sqrt2\,\dd$), a contradiction since $1\le 10/11$ is false.  The
argument realizes an unpublished 2019 sketch of J.~Parts; the contribution
here is a fully explicit, SAT-certified finite form with substantially
stronger constants ($5/11$ in place of $\tfrac12-\tfrac1{188}$), each
component carrying a DRAT proof trace checkable by an independent
$\sim$1300-line verifier, and the full $32$-point geometry certified in
exact symbolic arithmetic over explicit quadratic towers.  When this work was done (August 2026) the best published
bound for two distances was $6$; see the note added below for later
developments.

We further prove, with exact-arithmetic and doubly SAT-certified
verification: a $26$-vertex $6$-chromatic $\{1,\dd\}$-graph (the smallest
known $6$-chromatic two-distance graph for \emph{any} ratio, improving $31$
[Parts 2020] and, for this ratio, $117$ [De~Neve et al.\ 2025]); a
$30$-vertex $6$-chromatic $\{1,\sqrt3\}$-graph (previously $33$); the six-coloring continuum $X_6\supseteq[0.354,0.667504532081]$
(previously $[0.354,0.657]$); the edge
bound $e_5\le 2278$ for $5$-chromatic unit-distance graphs via an
edge-critical trimming of the $509$-vertex record graph (previously
$2406$); and forced-structure constants for invariant $5$-colorings of the
plane, including $p_{\sqrt3}\ge 1/12$ by an automated
linear-programming ``density ledger'' and the single-certificate bounds
$p_{\sqrt3}\ge 1/28$, $p_{\dd}\ge 1/37$.  Finally, we exhibit the first
color-count-free finite density caps at radii relevant to five colors
($p_{1/\sqrt3}\le 1/3$ by four points; $p_{(1+\sqrt5)/2}\le 2/5$ by the
unit pentagon; a dense family of capped radii from odd star polygons),
prove that pairwise-conflict mechanisms can never beat $1/2$ at the
ledger anchors $\sqrt3$ and $2$ (a Niven-type bipartiteness theorem),
and derive a crisp win condition: any $6$-chromatic $\{1,\sqrt3\}$-graph
forcing unit-edge monochromatic density above $1/3$ under
$\sqrt3$-proper $5$-colorings would prove $\chi(\R^2)\ge6$.  All
certificates and $\sim$60-line
verifiers are included in the reproducibility bundle
(Appendix~\ref{app:artifacts}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The Hadwiger--Nelson problem asks for the chromatic number of the plane,
$\chi(\R^2)=\chid{\{1\}}$: the least number of colors so that no two points
at distance $1$ share a color.  After more than half a century at
$4\le\chi(\R^2)\le 7$, de~Grey \cite{deGrey18} proved $\chi(\R^2)\ge 5$
with a $1581$-vertex unit-distance graph, launching the Polymath16 project
\cite{Polymath16} and a rapid sequence of smaller witnesses
\cite{Heule18,ExooIsmailescu18,Parts20b}.

In the \emph{two-distance} variant one forbids two distances
simultaneously: $\chid{\{1,\dd\}}$ is the chromatic number of the graph on
$\R^2$ with edges between points at distance $1$ \emph{or} $\dd$.  Finite
$6$-chromatic witnesses were known for exactly four ratios: the golden
ratio $(1+\sqrt5)/2$ (Huddleston 2008 \cite{Huddleston08},
non-explicit; explicit graphs and
the $31$-vertex record by Parts \cite{Parts20a}), $\dd=2$
(Exoo--Ismailescu \cite{ExooIsmailescu20}, reduced to $397$ vertices by
Parts), and $\dd=\sqrt3$ and $\dd=(\sqrt6+\sqrt2)/2$ (De~Neve, Vanden
Kerchove, Colle, Tavernier, and Pickavet \cite{DeNeve25}, with $33$ and
$117$ vertices respectively).  For no finite set $D$ of distances was a
bound $\chid{D}\ge 7$ published, although J.~Parts sketched a
probabilistic argument for exactly this in a 2019 Polymath16 comment
\cite{Parts19}.

\emph{Note added (October 2026).} After this work was done, OpenAI~\cite{OAI} proved $\chi(\R^2)\ge6$, so every two-distance chromatic number is now at least $6$ and the win condition of Section~\ref{sec:k5caps} is moot; the explicit small $6$-chromatic graphs of Section~\ref{sec:record} remain records. The transfer of~\cite{OAI} extends to finite sets of algebraic distances (see Crocker~\cite{Crocker} and our companion preprint~\cite{BassR3}), giving $\chid{D}\ge 1/m_D(\R^2)$; combined with Bukh's theorem this yields $\chid{\{1,N\}}\ge17$ for all sufficiently large $N$, with no explicit threshold. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} remains the only bound $\chid{D}\ge7$ for an explicit two-distance set that is proved by a finite certificate.

This paper reports the results of an intensive computer-assisted session
(2026-08-08/09).  Throughout, $\dd:=(\sqrt6+\sqrt2)/2=2\cos(\pi/12)\approx
1.93185$, whose relevant algebra is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identity}
  \dd^{\,2}=2+\sqrt3,
  \qquad
  \dd^{\,2}-1 \;=\; 1+\sqrt3 \;=\; \sqrt2\,\dd .
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Main theorem; invariant-coloring framework]\label{thm:main}
$\chid{\{1,\dd\}}\ge 7$.  More precisely, there is no proper $6$-coloring
$c\colon\R^2\to\{1,\dots,6\}$ avoiding monochromatic pairs at distances
$1$ and $\dd$: for any such $c$, invariant monochromatic-pair densities
$p_{\sqrt2}(c)$ and $p_{1+\sqrt3}(c)$ exist (via an invariant mean on the
isometry group) and satisfy the incompatible system
\[
  p_{\sqrt2}+p_{1+\sqrt3}\ \ge\ 1,
  \qquad
  p_{\sqrt2}\ \le\ \tfrac{5}{11},
  \qquad
  p_{1+\sqrt3}\ \le\ \tfrac{5}{11}.
\]
\end{theorem}

The three displayed inequalities come from three finite configurations
($8+12+12=32$ points in total), each verified by two independent SAT
solvers and certified by DRAT proof traces (Section~\ref{sec:seven};
Appendix~\ref{app:artifacts}).  The framework, its provenance in ZFC, and
its honest limitations are discussed in
Sections~\ref{sec:prelim}--\ref{sec:seven}; a counting blueprint that
would replace the framework by a single finite graph is given in
Section~\ref{sec:blueprint}.

The remaining results are classical finite statements with
exact-arithmetic certificates:

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:records}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item There are $26$ points in the plane spanning $85$ pairs at distance
exactly $1$ or exactly $\dd$ whose $\{1,\dd\}$-graph has chromatic number
$6$ and is vertex-critical.  This is the smallest known $6$-chromatic
two-distance graph for any distance pair (previously $31$
\cite{Parts20a}; for this pair, $117$ \cite{DeNeve25}).
\item There are $30$ points spanning $100$ pairs at distance exactly $1$
or exactly $\sqrt3$ whose graph has chromatic number $6$ and is
vertex-critical (previously $33$ \cite{DeNeve25}).
\item The record $509$-vertex $5$-chromatic unit-distance graph
\cite{Parts20b,deGreyParts22} contains an edge-critical spanning subgraph
with $2278$ edges that is not $4$-colorable.  Hence $e_5\le 2278$
(previously $e_5\le 2406$ \cite{deGreyParts22}).
\item In every isometry-invariant proper $5$-coloring of the plane (unit

program).  The best previously implicit constant, via Parts'
$397$-vertex $\{1,2\}$-graph, was of order $1/550$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

\subsection{Two-distance graphs}

For a finite $P\subset\R^2$ and a set $D$ of positive reals, let $G(P;D)$
be the graph on $P$ with $uv$ an edge iff $|u-v|\in D$.  We write
$\chid{D}$ for the chromatic number of the (infinite) graph $G(\R^2;D)$
and abbreviate $G(P)=G(P;\{1,\dd\})$ when $D=\{1,\dd\}$ is understood.
By the de~Bruijn--Erd\H{o}s theorem (with choice), $\chid{D}\ge k$
holds iff some finite $P$ has $\chi(G(P;D))\ge k$; part of the interest
of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is precisely that it does \emph{not} yet come
with such a finite witness (see Section~\ref{sec:caveat}).

All configurations in Sections~\ref{sec:record} have coordinates in a real
quadratic-tower field ($\Q(\sqrt3,\sqrt5)$ or $\Q(\sqrt3,\sqrt7)$) with
denominators dividing $8$, so squared distances are computed in exact
integer arithmetic and edge membership is decidable with no floating
point.  The $\chi\ge7$ configurations of Section~\ref{sec:seven} are
likewise exact algebraic configurations, certified end-to-end by
symbolic computation in explicit quadratic towers
(Section~\ref{sec:exact}).

\subsection{The invariant-coloring framework}\label{sec:framework}

We use the probabilistic formulation of Hadwiger--Nelson-type problems
developed by the Polymath16 project \cite{Polymath16}, \'Agoston
\cite{Agoston21}, and Gwyn--Stavrianos \cite{GwynStavrianos20}.  The
isometry group $E(2)=\Iso(\R^2)$ is solvable, hence amenable, so it
admits a left-invariant mean $\Lambda$ on $\ell^\infty(E(2))$ (this uses
Hahn--Banach; it is a theorem of ZFC).

\begin{definition}\label{def:pr}
Fix any proper coloring $c$ of $G(\R^2;D)$ with $k$ colors (no
measurability assumed) and fix an invariant mean $\Lambda$.  For $r>0$
define the \emph{monochromatic-pair density}
\[
  p_r \;=\; p_r(c,\Lambda)\;=\;
  \Lambda_{g\in E(2)}\Bigl[\,\mathbf 1\bigl(c(g\cdot(0,0))=c(g\cdot(r,0))\bigr)\Bigr]
  \ \in[0,1].
\]
\end{definition}

By left-invariance, for \emph{every} ordered pair $(x,y)$ with $|x-y|=r$
we have $\Lambda_g[\mathbf 1(c(gx)=c(gy))]=p_r$.  This yields the
transfer principle we use throughout:

\begin{lemma}[Finite transfer]\label{lem:transfer}
Let $Q\subset\R^2$ be finite with designated pairs
$(a_1,b_1),\dots,(a_m,b_m)$, $|a_i-b_i|=r_i$, and let $t_{\min}$
(resp.\ $t_{\max}$) be the minimum (resp.\ maximum), over all proper
$k$-colorings of the finite graph $G(Q;D)$, of the number of
monochromatic designated pairs.  Then for every proper $k$-coloring $c$
of $G(\R^2;D)$ and every invariant mean $\Lambda$,
\[
  t_{\min}\ \le\ \sum_{i=1}^{m} p_{r_i}(c,\Lambda)\ \le\ t_{\max}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For each $g\in E(2)$, the restriction of $c$ to $gQ$ is a proper
$k$-coloring of $G(gQ;D)\cong G(Q;D)$, so the number of monochromatic
designated pairs of $gQ$ lies in $[t_{\min},t_{\max}]$ pointwise in $g$.
Apply $\Lambda$ and use linearity together with the paragraph above,
which gives $\Lambda_g[\mathbf 1(c(ga_i)=c(gb_i))]=p_{r_i}$.
\end{proof}

Both $t_{\min}$ and $t_{\max}$ are computed exactly by maximum
satisfiability (MaxSAT) over the finite graph and certified as follows:
the optimum is found by an exact MaxSAT solver (RC2 over CaDiCaL), and
the corresponding decision statement (``a proper $k$-coloring with
$\ge t_{\max}+1$, resp.\ $\le t_{\min}-1$, monochromatic designated pairs
exists'') is refuted by CNF unsatisfiability with a DRAT proof trace
checked by \texttt{drat-trim} \cite{drattrim}.  Realizability of the
optimum is witnessed by an explicit coloring.

\section{Seven colors for two distances}\label{sec:seven}

Throughout this section $k=6$, $D=\{1,\dd\}$, and $c$ is an arbitrary
proper $6$-coloring of $G(\R^2;\{1,\dd\})$, assumed to exist for
contradiction; $p_r=p_r(c,\Lambda)$ for a fixed invariant mean.  The
two auxiliary distances are $\sqrt2$ and $1+\sqrt3$ --- not themselves
forbidden, but tied to $\{1,\dd\}$ by the identity \eqref{eq:identity}.

\subsection{The V8 pigeonhole}\label{sec:v8}

Parts' \emph{V8 gadget} consists of two concentric squares: the unit
square $I=\{(\pm\tfrac12,\pm\tfrac12)\}$ and the outer square
$O=\{(\pm s,0),(0,\pm s)\}$ with $s=\dd/\sqrt2=(1+\sqrt3)/2$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:v8geom}
$G(I\cup O;\{1,\dd\})=K_8$ minus the perfect matching consisting of the
four ``diagonals'': the two inner diagonals at distance $\sqrt2$ and the
two outer diagonals at distance $2s=1+\sqrt3$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Direct computation.  Inner sides have length $1$; inner diagonals
$\sqrt2$.  Outer sides have squared length $2s^2=2+\sqrt3=\dd^2$; outer
diagonals $2s=1+\sqrt3$.  For an inner corner and an outer vertex, e.g.\
$(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ and $(s,0)$: the difference is
$(\tfrac{\sqrt3}2,\tfrac12)$ of length $1$; and against $(-s,0)$ the
squared distance is $\bigl(\tfrac{2+\sqrt3}{2}\bigr)^2+\tfrac14
=2+\sqrt3=\dd^2$.  By symmetry each inner vertex is at distance $1$ from
the two nearest outer vertices and $\dd$ from the two farthest.  Thus all
$24$ pairs except the four diagonals are edges.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[V8 pigeonhole]\label{prop:v8}
Every proper $6$-coloring of $G(I\cup O;\{1,\dd\})$ makes at least $2$ of
the four diagonal pairs monochromatic.  The bound is exact, and all $11$
diagonal patterns of weight $\ge2$ are realizable.  Certificate:
\texttt{v8cert.cnf} (proper $6$-coloring $\wedge$ at most one
monochromatic diagonal; $84$ variables, $267$ clauses) is UNSAT, with
DRAT trace \texttt{v8cert.drat} verified by \texttt{drat-trim}.
\end{proposition}

Intuitively: six colors on $K_8$ minus a perfect matching leave at most
six ``private'' colors, so at least two matched pairs must share.
(The pigeonhole $8-6=2$ is tight because the four diagonal pairs are
disjoint.)  By Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer} with the four diagonals as
designated pairs (two at $\sqrt2$, two at $1+\sqrt3$) and $t_{\min}=2$:

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:v8}
$2p_{\sqrt2}+2p_{1+\sqrt3}\ \ge\ 2$, i.e.\
$p_{\sqrt2}+p_{1+\sqrt3}\ \ge\ 1$.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{The chain configurations and the exact identity}\label{sec:chains}

The counterweight to Corollary~\ref{cor:v8} is a pair of $12$-point
configurations bounding $p_{\sqrt2}$ and $p_{1+\sqrt3}$ from above.
Fix a radius $r\in\{\sqrt2,\,1+\sqrt3\}$ and let
\[
  \varphi_1=2\arcsin\frac{1}{2r},\qquad
  \varphi_{\dd}=2\arcsin\frac{\dd}{2r}
\]
be the angles subtending chords of length $1$ and $\dd$ on a circle of
radius $r$; equivalently $\cos\varphi_1=1-\tfrac1{2r^2}$ and
$\cos\varphi_{\dd}=1-\tfrac{\dd^2}{2r^2}$.  Concretely,
\[
  r=\sqrt2:\ \ \cos\varphi_1=\tfrac34,\ \
  \cos\varphi_{\dd}=\tfrac{2-\sqrt3}{4};
  \qquad
  r=1+\sqrt3:\ \ \cos\varphi_1=\tfrac{2+\sqrt3}{4},\ \
  \cos\varphi_{\dd}=\tfrac34,
\]
a pleasing duality: the two radii exchange the roles of the two cosines.

Set $A=\varphi_{\dd}-\varphi_1$ and $B=\varphi_{\dd}+\varphi_1$, and
define the \emph{mixed chain} $X_0,\dots,X_6$ by $X_0=(0,0)$ and
$X_{i+1}=X_i+r(\cos h_i,\sin h_i)$, where the heading sequence has period
four:
\[
  (h_0,h_1,h_2,h_3)\;=\;\bigl(0,\ \pi-A,\ -(A+B),\ \pi-B\bigr),
  \qquad h_{i+4}=h_i .
\]
Thus consecutive chain points are at distance exactly $r$.  The key fact
is that the four-step displacement lands \emph{exactly} on a forbidden
distance:

\begin{lemma}[Four-step closure]\label{lem:closure}
For all $i$, $X_{i+4}-X_i$ is the same vector, of length
$(\dd^2-1)/r$.  Consequently, by \eqref{eq:identity}:
for $r=\sqrt2$, $|X_{i+4}-X_i|=(1+\sqrt3)/\sqrt2=\dd$;
for $r=1+\sqrt3$, $|X_{i+4}-X_i|=1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Identifying $\R^2$ with $\mathbb C$,
$X_{i+4}-X_i=r\sum_{j=i}^{i+3}e^{\mathrm i h_j}
=r\sum_{j=0}^{3}e^{\mathrm i h_j}$, since the headings have period four.
Now
\[
  \sum_{j=0}^{3}e^{\mathrm i h_j}
  \;=\;1-e^{-\mathrm iA}+e^{-\mathrm i(A+B)}-e^{-\mathrm iB}
  \;=\;\bigl(1-e^{-\mathrm iA}\bigr)\bigl(1-e^{-\mathrm iB}\bigr),
\]
whose modulus is $4\sin\tfrac A2\sin\tfrac B2
=2\bigl(\cos\tfrac{B-A}2-\cos\tfrac{B+A}2\bigr)
=2(\cos\varphi_1-\cos\varphi_{\dd})
=2\cdot\tfrac{\dd^2-1}{2r^2}=\tfrac{\dd^2-1}{r^2}$.
Multiplying by $r$ gives $|X_{i+4}-X_i|=(\dd^2-1)/r$.
\end{proof}

So for $r=\sqrt2$ the pairs $(X_i,X_{i+4})$, $i=0,1,2$, are genuine
$\dd$-edges of the two-distance graph, and for $r=1+\sqrt3$ they are unit
edges: the chain's long-range structure is policed by the forbidden
distances even though its steps are not.  To stiffen the configuration we
add five \emph{apexes} $C_1,\dots,C_5$: $C_i$ is the rotation of
$X_{i-1}$ about $X_i$ through $\pm\varphi_1$ (one branch per $i$).  Then
$|C_iX_i|=r$ and $|C_iX_{i-1}|=1$ automatically, and the branch is chosen
so that $|C_iX_{i+1}|=\dd$ --- exactly, for every $i$, at both radii
(Section~\ref{sec:exact}); the apexes are distinct from all chain
points.  The resulting configuration
$Q_r=\{X_0,\dots,X_6,C_1,\dots,C_5\}$ has $12$ points; its
$\{1,\dd\}$-graph has $14$ edges ($5$ unit and $9$ at distance $\dd$ for
$r=\sqrt2$; $9$ and $5$ respectively for $r=1+\sqrt3$).  The
\emph{charged slots} are the eleven designated pairs at distance $r$:
\[
  (X_i,X_{i+1})\ \ (0\le i\le 5)
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  (X_i,C_i)\ \ (1\le i\le 5).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Chain caps]\label{prop:caps}
For $r=\sqrt2$ (chain $A$) and for $r=1+\sqrt3$ (chain $B$): in every
proper $6$-coloring of $G(Q_r;\{1,\dd\})$, at most $5$ of the $11$
charged slots are monochromatic, and $5$ is attained.  Certificates:
\texttt{capA.cnf} and \texttt{capB.cnf} (proper $6$-coloring $\wedge$ at
least $6$ monochromatic slots; $188$ variables, $646$ clauses each) are
UNSAT with DRAT traces \texttt{capA.drat}, \texttt{capB.drat} verified by
\texttt{drat-trim}; the optima were computed by MaxSAT (RC2/CaDiCaL) and
confirmed by exhaustive enumeration --- on the \emph{exact} edge lists of
Section~\ref{sec:exact}.
\end{proposition}

By Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer} with the eleven slots (all at distance $r$)
and $t_{\max}=5$:

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:caps}
$11\,p_{\sqrt2}\le 5$ and $11\,p_{1+\sqrt3}\le 5$; that is,
$p_{\sqrt2}\le \tfrac5{11}$ and $p_{1+\sqrt3}\le\tfrac5{11}$.
\end{corollary}

This is, to our knowledge, the first \emph{finite strict-half} bound
$p_r\le\tfrac12-\varepsilon$ established for any number of colors
$k\ge5$: a single $12$-point configuration forcing slot mono-density
$\le\tfrac5{11}=\tfrac12-\tfrac1{22}$.  Parts' sketch \cite{Parts19}
obtains $p_r<\tfrac12$ by counting along arbitrarily long zigzag chains;
our reconstruction of that argument yields $p_r\le\tfrac12-\tfrac1{188}$
(plain zigzag, chain constant $C(6)=94$), improved to
$\tfrac12-\tfrac1{146}$ by the mixed-heading word above, while the finite
$12$-point form with apexes reaches $\tfrac12-\tfrac1{22}$ and is exactly
optimal for its configuration.

\subsection{Exact-arithmetic certification of the chains}\label{sec:exact}

Every geometric statement about $Q_{\sqrt2}$ (chain $A$) and
$Q_{1+\sqrt3}$ (chain $B$) --- including the apex placements --- has been
verified in exact symbolic arithmetic; no floating point enters the
$\chi\ge7$ certificates.  The verifier is
\texttt{exactverify/verify\_exact.py} ($\sim$250 lines, on
\texttt{sympy} $+$ \texttt{python-sat}); its design:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Towers.}  All coordinates of chain $A$ lie in
$K_A=\Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3,\sqrt7,S)$ with $S=\sqrt{9+4\sqrt3}$
(note $\sin\varphi_{\dd}=S/4$, $\sin\varphi_1=\sqrt7/4$ at $r=\sqrt2$),
a tower of nominal degree $16$; those of chain $B$ lie in
$K_B=\Q(\sqrt3,\sqrt7,T)$ with $T=\sqrt{9-4\sqrt3}$
(here $\sin\varphi_1=T/4$, $\sin\varphi_{\dd}=\sqrt7/4$), of degree $8$.
Elements are polynomials in the generators, reduced to normal form
modulo the defining relations $S^2=9+4\sqrt3$, $w_2^2=2$, $w_7^2=7$,
$w_3^2=3$ (resp.\ $T^2=9-4\sqrt3$, \dots).  Under a lexicographic order
the relation set has pairwise-coprime pure-square leading monomials,
hence is a Gr\"obner basis, so normal forms are unique and the quotient
ring has dimension $16$ (resp.\ $8$).
\item \emph{Soundness certificate.}  Nonzero normal form certifies a
nonzero field element only if the quotient ring \emph{is} the field.
This is certified separately: the minimal polynomial of the primitive
element $\sqrt2+\sqrt3+\sqrt7+\sqrt{9+4\sqrt3}$ (resp.\
$\sqrt3+\sqrt7+\sqrt{9-4\sqrt3}$) is computed and its degree confirmed
to be $16$ (resp.\ $8$).  Every subsequent equality/inequality decision
is therefore exact and unconditional.
\item \emph{Checks.}  For each chain: the headings are exact unit
vectors; all six steps satisfy $|X_iX_{i+1}|^2=r^2$ exactly; the five
apexes satisfy $|C_iX_i|^2=r^2$, $|C_iX_{i-1}|^2=1$, and
$|C_iX_{i+1}|^2=\dd^2=2+\sqrt3$ exactly; the three long pairs satisfy
$|X_iX_{i+4}|^2=(\dd^2-1)^2/r^2$ exactly ($=2+\sqrt3$ for chain $A$,
$=1$ for chain $B$), confirming Lemma~\ref{lem:closure} independently;
and \emph{all} $66$ vertex pairs are classified exactly, reproducing
the $14$-edge sets used by the SAT certificates and confirming that all
$11$ charged slots are exactly at distance $r$ and are non-edges.  The
exact coordinates agree with the published float coordinates
(\texttt{chain12.json}, \texttt{chain12b.json}) to $10^{-9}$.
\item \emph{Caps on exact edges.}  Proposition~\ref{prop:caps} is
re-proved on the exact edge lists by exhaustive enumeration: all
$\binom{11}{6}=462$ six-subsets of the slots are unsatisfiable as
simultaneous monochromatic sets under proper $6$-coloring, while a
five-subset is satisfiable; hence the maximum is exactly $5$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Suppose $c$ is a proper $6$-coloring of $G(\R^2;\{1,\dd\})$ and fix an
invariant mean $\Lambda$ (ZFC).  By Corollary~\ref{cor:v8},
$p_{\sqrt2}+p_{1+\sqrt3}\ge1$; by Corollary~\ref{cor:caps},
$p_{\sqrt2}+p_{1+\sqrt3}\le\tfrac{10}{11}$.  Contradiction.  Hence no
proper $6$-coloring exists and $\chid{\{1,\dd\}}\ge7$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The framework caveat, honestly}\label{sec:caveat}

Two caveats separate Theorem~\ref{thm:main} from a classical
finite-witness bound such as de~Grey's $\chi(\R^2)\ge5$.  (A third ---
floating-point placement of the chain apexes --- was eliminated by the
exact-arithmetic certification of Section~\ref{sec:exact}: the V8 and
both chains are now verified exactly, end to end.)

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Nonconstructive averaging.}  The densities $p_r$ exist by an
invariant mean on the amenable group $E(2)$, whose existence uses
Hahn--Banach.  This is a ZFC theorem, so Theorem~\ref{thm:main} rules
out \emph{arbitrary} (not merely measurable) $6$-colorings in ZFC.  But
in ZF+DC the mean need not exist, and Shelah--Soifer
\cite{ShelahSoifer03} showed chromatic numbers of distance graphs can
genuinely depend on the ambient set theory.  The choice-free content of
this section is exactly its three finite certificates.
\item \emph{No explicit finite witness yet.}  By the de~Bruijn--Erd\H{o}s
theorem a finite $7$-chromatic $\{1,\dd\}$-graph exists, but the argument
does not exhibit one.  Section~\ref{sec:blueprint} makes the
gap precise, reduces it to a combinatorial design problem --- and
Section~\ref{sec:verdict} shows the most obvious design vocabulary is
arithmetically obstructed.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Toward a fully finite witness}\label{sec:blueprint}

\subsection{The counting blueprint}

The two halves of the proof are pointwise statements about finite
configurations; only the step from ``every placement'' to ``density''
uses the invariant mean.  A single finite graph would suffice if the
slots of many chain copies could be made to \emph{coincide} with the
diagonals of many V8 copies:

\begin{proposition}[Uniform-multiplicity blueprint]\label{prop:blueprint}
Suppose a finite point set $W$ contains $N$ V8 copies and a family
$\mathcal C$ of chain copies (of types $A$ and $B$) such that the
multiset of charged slots of $\mathcal C$ covers the $4N$ diagonal pairs
of the V8s, each with the same multiplicity $m\ge1$, and contains no
other pairs.  Then $G(W;\{1,\dd\})$ is not $6$-colorable.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $M$ be the number of monochromatic diagonals in a putative proper
$6$-coloring.  Summing Proposition~\ref{prop:v8} over the $N$ V8s gives
$M\ge2N$.  Each chain contributes $11$ slots of which at most $5$ are
monochromatic (Proposition~\ref{prop:caps}); summing over $\mathcal C$
and using the uniform multiplicity, $mM\le\tfrac5{11}\cdot 4Nm$, i.e.\
$M\le\tfrac{20}{11}N<2N$.  Contradiction.
\end{proof}

The design problem is thus to realize such an incidence structure with
actual plane geometry --- every slot at distance $\sqrt2$ or $1+\sqrt3$
being simultaneously a diagonal of some unit or outer square whose
V8 completion is present.  A relaxed, defect-tolerant version was worked
out in the session memos:

\begin{theorem}[Assembly theorem; choice-free finitization --- announced]
\label{thm:assembly}
Let a configuration consist of $N$ copies of each chain family with slot
weights $w_e=(4,8,9,9,8,4)$ on edge slots and $w_a=(5,7,7,7,5)$ on apex
slots (total weight $73$), together with reflection conflict-pairings
with defect $\eta$ and V8 completions on all slots with goodness defect
$\theta$.  If
\[
  99\,\theta+111\,\eta<1,
\]
the configuration is not $6$-colorable.  No amenability and no choice are
used.
\end{theorem}

\noindent
The proof (a weighted double count refining
Proposition~\ref{prop:blueprint}) lives in the session memos and should
be written out in full before publication.  %% TODO: write up the proof
  %% of Theorem 4.2 from the session memos.
The catch is quantitative: a naive faithful closure of the required
incidences is F\o lner-dominated and balloons to roughly $10^{40}$
vertices unless local \emph{rational-angle cap gadgets} exist.  This
motivates:

\begin{question}[Gatekeeper question]\label{q:gatekeeper}
Does a finite $\{1,\dd\}$-graph exist containing two designated $r$-pairs
in a rational-angle relative position such that no proper $6$-coloring
makes both pairs monochromatic?  The sweet spot is ``not both diagonals
of one unit square monochromatic'' ($90^\circ$, self-supplied by V8s).
\end{question}

The answer controls whether the finite witness has $\sim10^3$ or
$\sim10^{40}$ vertices.  Current evidence is negative at small scale:
in a $1837$-point, $16{,}000$-edge closure of the $\zeta_{24}$ lattice
(a $12$-fold wheel of De~Neve-style $S$-graphs about a hub, whose output
ring is a regular $12$-gon with side exactly $\sqrt2$ and $60^\circ$
chords exactly $1+\sqrt3$, hence self-seeding), colorings making both
diagonals of a designated unit square monochromatic exist and are found
quickly.  The same test inside the full $24{,}881$-point universe is
also negative: growing neighborhoods of a designated unit square through
eight rounds (to $1837$ points and $16{,}116$ edges) never destroys
both-diagonals-monochromatic $6$-colorings.  Within the tested horizon,
no rational-angle cap gadget exists.

\subsection{Obstruction theorems}

The session also established, by exhaustive SAT analysis over the
$6$-color $S$-graph calculus, that several cheaper routes are closed:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Multiplicity-1:} two copies of a configuration yield at most
one useful clause; naive duplication does not amplify.
\item \emph{Trees never force:} tree-like slot incidence structures
cannot force a monochromatic pair.
\item \emph{No odd spindle cycles} exist among the relevant angles: no
integer relation of weight $\le7$ holds among
$\alpha=\arccos\tfrac{2+\sqrt3}4$, $\beta=\arccos\tfrac34$,
$\gamma=\arccos\tfrac{2-\sqrt3}4$,
$\delta'=\arccos\tfrac{3\sqrt6-\sqrt2}8$, and $30^\circ$ (verified
computationally).
\item \emph{Two-V8 sharing is logically transparent:} for two V8s
sharing $4$ points, all $121=11^2$ joint weight-$\ge2$ diagonal patterns
are feasible; pattern-level (NAND-calculus) reasoning over V8 arrays
cannot force anything.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Consequently color-flow arguments or chain-cap gadgets (as in
Section~\ref{sec:chains}) are \emph{mandatory} ingredients for any
finite witness; there is no purely pattern-logical shortcut.

\subsection{The design-problem verdict: an arithmetic obstruction}
\label{sec:verdict}

A second wave of computations settled whether the chain/V8 vocabulary
\emph{by itself} can realize the blueprint.  The verdict is negative,
for structural rather than search-depth reasons.  All computations below
are exact (symbolic module arithmetic, exhaustive SAT pattern
enumeration); their full write-up is pending.
%% TODO: the coincidence scans, rank computations, and dual LP live in
%% the session archive (design7/); write up proofs before publication.

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Complete pattern polytopes.}  The exact sets of achievable
monochromatic-slot patterns were enumerated for the V8 (all patterns of
weight $\ge2$, none below) and for both chains (all achievable subsets
of the $11$ slots).  These sets determine \emph{every} valid linear
inequality available to any counting argument built from these gadgets.
\item \emph{Trimmed caps.}  Restricting chain $A$ to the $7$-slot
subfamily $\{1,2,3,4,7,8,9\}$ yields cap $3/7$; exhaustive search over
all $2047$ nonempty slot subsets shows $3/7$ is the best (lowest)
achievable cap ratio of any trimmed chain gadget.
\item \emph{Max-1-cross.}  Exact coincidence scans over the full hinge
family ($A$--$A$, $B$--$B$, and the $A$--$B$ $45^\circ$ hinges) show
that two chain placements can share at most \emph{one} charged slot,
and the resulting cross-incidence graphs are bipartite.  Slot
multiplicity therefore cannot be accumulated by crossing chains.
\item \emph{Placement-module freeness.}  The translation modules
governing periodic ladder assemblies have full $\Z$-rank --- verified
exactly over $\Q(\sqrt3,\sqrt7)$ and the towers $K_A$, $K_B$ of
Section~\ref{sec:exact}, including the joint $A$--$B$ ``marriage''
modules under all rotation and reflection branches.  The arithmetic
admits no hidden rational relations, so faithful designs blow up
combinatorially (box designs of size roughly $(cr)^r$ in the rank $r$).
\item \emph{Tree defect.}  Within the assembly framework of
Theorem~\ref{thm:assembly}, tree-shaped (acyclic-incidence) designs
provably cannot win: the counting inequality tolerates a total defect
budget of $1/14$, while the best tree designs leak on the order of
$90\%$.
\item \emph{Dual emptiness.}  A Farkas-dual linear program over the
exact pattern polytopes characterizes precisely which gadget
inventories can certify non-$6$-colorability of a finite assembly.
With the current inventory (V8s, chains, trimmed chains, and their
weighted combinations) subject to the coincidence constraints above,
the dual is empty: \emph{no} assembly of these gadgets alone, at any
scale, yields a finite contradiction.
\end{itemize}

Three escape routes survive, in order of assessed promise:
(i) \emph{generic-position pigeonhole gadgets} --- V8-style pigeonholes
that operate in generic relative position, bypassing the coincidence
arithmetic entirely (this is also the direction of
Question~\ref{q:gatekeeper});
(ii) the \emph{rank-one marriage door}: rational-norm vectors in the
$A$-module whose squared lengths match $B$-side slot forms, the single
coincidence channel the freeness computation leaves open; and
(iii) \emph{replacement cap gadgets at new radii}, e.g.\ from the
star-polygon family of Section~\ref{sec:k5caps}, re-anchoring the count
away from $\sqrt2$ and $1+\sqrt3$.

\subsection{Method: SAT-guided iterated local search over module
closures}\label{sec:method}

Both graphs were found by the same pipeline, seeded from the published
coordinate tables of De~Neve et al.\ \cite{DeNeve25} (their $33$-vertex
$\{1,\sqrt3\}$-graph and $117$-vertex $\{1,\dd\}$-graph).

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Universe.}  A bounded-coefficient slice of the $\Z$-module
closure of the seed coordinates: all points $p+q-r$ over the current
point set staying in the same coset of the coordinate module (for
$\{1,\sqrt3\}$: $\{((a_0+a_1\sqrt5)/8,(a_2\sqrt3+a_3\sqrt{15})/8):
a_i\in\Z,\ a_0\equiv a_1,\ a_2\equiv a_3\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)\}$,
$\approx10^4$ points within radius~$3.4$).  The closure contains
coefficient patterns the seed constructions never generate; the
$26$-vertex graph uses several such points.
\item \emph{Oracle.}  An incremental CaDiCaL instance with per-vertex
selector assumptions answers ``is this subset still $6$-chromatic?'' in
microseconds.
\item \emph{Search.}  Randomized greedy vertex deletion descends to
critical subgraphs; perturbation kicks (adjacency-weighted candidate
insertion, remove-$k$, re-descend) escape local minima.  The $30$-vertex
minimum was reached within $\sim10^4$ oracle calls ($\approx1$ minute on
an Apple M4~Pro); it shares $23$ vertices with the $33$-vertex seed,
omits $10$, and adds $7$ new.  The $117\to26$ collapse additionally
exploited a \emph{pinned-anchor artifact}: the search's symmetry-breaking
clique had been exempt from deletion, and an unpinned post-pass cascaded
$29\to26$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Verification recipe}\label{sec:verify}

Every claim in Theorems~\ref{thm:g26} and~\ref{thm:g30} is re-derivable
from the integer vector tables alone:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Exact edges.}  Recompute all pairwise squared distances in
integer arithmetic as above; no floating point enters any certificate.
\item \emph{Non-$5$-colorability.}  The direct $130$-- resp.\
$150$-variable CNF (one variable per vertex-color pair, no
symmetry-breaking assumptions) is UNSAT, proven independently by CaDiCaL
1.9.5 and Glucose 4.2, and certified by DRAT traces
(\texttt{record26.drat}: an $18{,}518$-step refutation;
\texttt{record30.drat} likewise) checked with \texttt{drat-trim}
(\texttt{s VERIFIED}).
\item \emph{$6$-colorability and criticality.}  Explicit $6$-colorings
were found by SAT; all single-vertex-deleted subgraphs are $5$-colorable
(explicit colorings).
\item \emph{Standalone verifiers.}  \texttt{verify30.py} and
\texttt{verify117best.py} ($\sim60$ lines each; dependency:
\texttt{python-sat}) recompute the edge sets exactly from the integer
vectors and re-solve everything from scratch in seconds.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}
Whether a $29$-vertex (or smaller) $6$-chromatic two-distance graph
exists for any ratio is open.  Note that the bound $v_6\ge42$ for
unit-distance graphs \cite{deGreyParts22} does not apply to two-distance
graphs, where only trivial lower bounds are known for $\{1,\sqrt3\}$;
the $26$-vertex graph is vertex-critical and survived extended attack.
\end{remark}

\section{Unit-distance results}\label{sec:ud}

\subsection{$e_5\le2278$}\label{sec:e5}

The smallest known $5$-chromatic unit-distance graph has $509$ vertices
and $2442$ edges \cite{Parts20b,deGreyParts22}.  It was known not to be
edge-critical: de~Grey and Parts removed $36$ edges non-exhaustively,
giving $e_5\le2406$, and noted room for improvement
\cite{deGreyParts22}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:e5}
The $509$-vertex graph contains a spanning subgraph with $2278$ edges
($164$ removed) that is not $4$-colorable and is edge-critical: removing
any single further edge admits a proper $4$-coloring.  Hence
$e_5\le2278$.
\end{theorem}

The trim used the same incremental selector-assumption technique as
Section~\ref{sec:method}, over edges instead of vertices: repeatedly
attempt to delete a random edge, keeping the deletion iff the remainder
is still $4$-uncolorable, until no edge is removable.  The final
$2278$-edge set is certified non-$4$-colorable by a DRAT trace
(\texttt{edge2278.cnf}/\texttt{edge2278.drat}, checked with
\texttt{drat-trim}); criticality is witnessed by explicit $4$-colorings
of all $2278$ single-edge deletions.  A second, independently seeded run
terminated at $2279$ edges, indicating the greedy endpoint is
order-dependent; the true $e_5$ may be lower still.

\subsection{Forced structure in $5$-colorings of the plane}\label{sec:forced}

The transfer principle of Section~\ref{sec:framework} applies equally
with $k=5$ and $D=\{1\}$: a non-$5$-colorable $\{1,\dd'\}$-graph whose
$m$ $\dd'$-edges cannot all be avoided forces monochromatic pairs at
distance $\dd'$ in \emph{every} proper unit-distance $5$-coloring of the
plane (cf.\ Gwyn--Stavrianos \cite{GwynStavrianos20}).

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:forced}
In every proper $5$-coloring of $G(\R^2;\{1\})$, with $p_r$ the invariant
densities of Definition~\ref{def:pr}:
\[
  p_{\sqrt3}\ \ge\ \tfrac1{28},
  \qquad
  p_{\dd}\ \ge\ \tfrac1{37}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Consider the $30$-point set of Theorem~\ref{thm:g30}.  Any $5$-coloring
of the plane proper at distance $1$ restricts to a coloring of the $30$
points proper on the $72$ unit edges; since the full graph is not
$5$-colorable, at least $t=1$ of the $28$ $\sqrt3$-pairs is
monochromatic.  (MaxSAT shows $t=1$ exactly: two cannot be forced.)  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer}, $28\,p_{\sqrt3}\ge1$.  The same argument on
the $26$-point set (Theorem~\ref{thm:g26}; $37$ $\dd$-pairs, $t=1$)
gives $37\,p_{\dd}\ge1$.
\end{proof}

These are the strongest known single-certificate forced-structure
constants for $5$-colorings; the previous implicit best, via Parts'
$397$-vertex $\{1,2\}$-graph, was of order $1/550$.  Conversely
$p_{\dd'}\le\tfrac12$ for all $\dd'\ge\tfrac12$ by a color-count-free
spindle argument, so any construction achieving $t/m>\tfrac12$ would
prove $\chi(\R^2)\ge6$; the $t/m$ ladder is a quantitative measure of
progress toward the fundamental lower bound, and
Section~\ref{sec:k5caps} lowers the winning threshold from $\tfrac12$
to $\tfrac13$ at the right radius.  (The pursuit of small $m$
is what produced the $26$-vertex graph: the session's minimization runs
plateaued at $m=37$ $\dd$-edges.)

\subsection{An automated density ledger and $p_{\sqrt3}\ge1/12$}
\label{sec:ledger}

Single certificates are only the first rung.  The session built an
automated \emph{density ledger}: approximately $143{,}000$ MaxSAT-exact
linear inequalities over monochromatic-pair densities, mined from small
unit-rigid configurations (each inequality of the form
$\sum_i \alpha_i p_{r_i}\ge t$ or $\le t$, certified as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer}), coupled with triangle relations among
densities and solved as a linear program.

\begin{theorem}[Density ledger --- announced]\label{thm:ledger}
For every isometry-invariant proper $5$-coloring of the plane,
$p_{\sqrt3}\ \ge\ \tfrac1{12}$.
The LP also reproduces the classical hexagonal-packing inequality as a
special case.
\end{theorem}

The LP is feasible --- the ledger is a quantitative map of how much
freedom $5$-colorings retain --- and the framework turns ``prove
$\chi(\R^2)\ge6$'' into the concrete task ``find configurations whose
inequalities make this LP infeasible.''

\subsection{The $5$-coloring wall, measured}\label{sec:wall}

Is any \emph{single} distance forced monochromatic inside an actual
$5$-chromatic graph?  A sweep over Heule's $803$-vertex $5$-chromatic
graph \cite{Heule18} tested, for each of the $428$ distance classes with
at least $40$ point pairs, whether some proper $5$-coloring avoids
monochromatic pairs in that class entirely.  The answer is yes for every
class --- no forced single-distance family exists in this graph --- but
the classes are far from equal: every class resolved in milliseconds to
seconds, except $d=2$, which required $3.4$ hours of solver time before
a witness coloring was found.  Distance $2$ --- precisely the distance
the two-distance theory singles out (Exoo--Ismailescu's $\{1,2\}$
six-chromatic construction, and $\dd^2-1$-type coincidences) --- is
where the $5$-coloring solution space is thinnest.

\section{Strict caps for five colors and a win condition}\label{sec:k5caps}

\emph{(Superseded: this section was written before~\cite{OAI} proved $\chi(\R^2)\ge6$. We keep it for the cap theorems, which remain valid.)}

Corollary~\ref{cor:caps} caps a density strictly below $\tfrac12$ for
$6$-colorings.  This section develops the analogous theory on the
unit-distance side: caps valid for colorings with \emph{any} number of
colors, an impossibility theorem explaining where such caps cannot
exist, and the resulting explicit win condition for $\chi(\R^2)\ge6$.
Throughout, ``proper'' means proper at distance $1$, and $p_r$ is the
invariant density of Definition~\ref{def:pr} for such a coloring.

\subsection{Color-count-free finite caps}

\begin{proposition}[Triangle and circumcenter]\label{prop:cap13}
Let $Q$ consist of a unit equilateral triangle $v_1v_2v_3$ and its
circumcenter $o$, with $|o-v_i|=1/\sqrt3$.  In every proper coloring
(any number of colors), at most one of the three pairs $(o,v_i)$ is
monochromatic.  Hence $p_{1/\sqrt3}\le\tfrac13$ for every proper
coloring of the plane.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The $v_i$ are pairwise at distance $1$, so they receive three distinct
colors; $o$ can agree with at most one of them.  Transfer
(Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer}) gives $3\,p_{1/\sqrt3}\le1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Unit pentagon]\label{prop:cap25}
Let $\tau=(1+\sqrt5)/2$.  The regular pentagon with unit sides has
diagonals of length $\tau$; in every proper coloring, at most two of
the five diagonals are monochromatic, so $p_{\tau}\le\tfrac25$.
Dually, the regular pentagon with unit diagonals has sides of length
$1/\tau$ and caps $p_{1/\tau}\le\tfrac25$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Label the vertices $v_0,\dots,v_4$ cyclically.  If two diagonals
sharing a vertex, say $(v_0,v_2)$ and $(v_2,v_4)$, were both
monochromatic, then $c(v_0)=c(v_4)$, contradicting the unit side
$(v_4,v_0)$.  So monochromatic diagonals form an independent set in the
diagonal $5$-cycle $v_0v_2v_4v_1v_3$, of size at most $2$.  The dual
statement is identical with sides and diagonals exchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Odd stars and flowers]\label{prop:capstar}
For odd $n$ and $1\le a<n/2$ with $\gcd(a,n)=1$: place a regular
$n$-gon so that its step-$2a$ chords have length $1$; its step-$a$
chords then have length $r=1/(2\cos(a\pi/n))$, and in every proper
coloring at most $(n-1)/2$ of the $n$ step-$a$ chords are
monochromatic: $p_r\le\tfrac{n-1}{2n}<\tfrac12$.  Similarly the
``flower'' (regular $m$-gon with unit sides plus its center, $m$ odd)
caps $p_r\le\tfrac{m-1}{2m}$ at the circumradius $r=1/(2\sin(\pi/m))$;
$m=3$ recovers Proposition~\ref{prop:cap13}.  The capped radii
$1/(2\cos(a\pi/n))$ are dense in $(\tfrac12,\infty)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Two step-$a$ chords sharing a vertex, $(v_i,v_{i+a})$ and
$(v_{i+a},v_{i+2a})$, cannot both be monochromatic: that would force
$c(v_i)=c(v_{i+2a})$ across the unit step-$2a$ chord.  So monochromatic
step-$a$ chords are independent in the step-$a$ cycle, which is a
single \emph{odd} cycle since $\gcd(a,n)=1$ and $n$ is odd; an odd
$n$-cycle has independence number $(n-1)/2$.  The flower argument is
the same with spokes in place of chords.
\end{proof}

These are, to our knowledge, the first finite sub-half density caps
relevant to $k\ge5$ --- and they are stronger than needed: they hold
for arbitrary proper colorings, with no bound on the number of colors.

\subsection{An impossibility theorem at the ledger anchors}

Why do the caps above live at $1/\sqrt3$, $\tau$, $1/(2\cos(a\pi/n))$
and not at the density-ledger anchors $\sqrt3$ and $2$ where they are
most wanted?  All of them run on the same fuel: two $r$-slots sharing
an endpoint conflict when their far endpoints are at distance $1$, and
the angle subtended at the shared endpoint is then
$\varphi_1(r)=2\arcsin\frac1{2r}$, with
$\cos\varphi_1=1-\tfrac1{2r^2}$.  A cap needs \emph{odd} conflict
cycles, i.e.\ closed walks of such conflicts --- and those exist only
when $\varphi_1$ is commensurable with $\pi$
(at $r=1/\sqrt3$: $\cos\varphi_1=-\tfrac12$, $\varphi_1=2\pi/3$; at
$r=\tau$: $\cos\varphi_1=\tau/2$, $\varphi_1=\pi/5$).

\begin{theorem}[Pairwise impossibility at $\sqrt3$ and $2$ ---
announced]\label{thm:impossible}
At $r=\sqrt3$ and $r=2$ the conflict angle has rational cosine
($\cos\varphi_1=\tfrac56$ and $\tfrac78$ respectively), so by Niven's
theorem \cite{Niven56} $\varphi_1$ is an irrational multiple of $\pi$.
Consequently all conflict cycles are even, every pairwise-conflict
graph on $r$-slots is bipartite, and the optimal pairwise cap is
exactly $\tfrac12$: no finite configuration whose only mechanism is
pairwise slot conflict can prove $p_{\sqrt3}<\tfrac12$ or
$p_2<\tfrac12$.
\end{theorem}

The bipartite bound is tight in practice: SAT verification through
$45$-point scaffolds, and exhaustive chain scans at $r=\sqrt3$ and
$r=2$ (all apex/pinch turn words, with and without waist points), all
plateau at density exactly $\tfrac12$ --- while the same scans at
$r=1/\sqrt3$ immediately break $\tfrac12$ (best chain found: $3/8$).
Beating $\tfrac12$ at $\sqrt3$ or $2$ therefore requires non-pairwise,
de~Grey-scale machinery.

\subsection{The win condition}

The caps convert record-graph engineering into a quantitative attack on
$\chi(\R^2)\ge6$.

\begin{theorem}[Win condition]\label{thm:win}
Let $G$ be a finite $\{1,\sqrt3\}$-graph with $m$ unit edges such that
every $5$-coloring of its vertices that is proper on the
$\sqrt3$-edges makes at least $t$ unit edges monochromatic (any
$6$-chromatic $\{1,\sqrt3\}$-graph has $t\ge1$).  If
$t/m>\tfrac13$, then $\chi(\R^2)\ge6$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $c$ is a proper $5$-coloring of $G(\R^2;\{1\})$.  Scale $G$ by
$1/\sqrt3$: its $\sqrt3$-edges become unit pairs and its unit edges
become pairs at distance $1/\sqrt3$.  For every isometric placement of
the scaled vertex set, $c$ restricted to it is a $5$-coloring proper on
the former $\sqrt3$-edges, so by hypothesis at least $t$ of the $m$
former unit edges are monochromatic.  Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer} gives
$p_{1/\sqrt3}\ge t/m>\tfrac13$, contradicting
Proposition~\ref{prop:cap13}.  Hence no proper $5$-coloring exists.
\end{proof}

The current certified value of the ratio is $t/m=1/72$: the $30$-vertex
graph of Theorem~\ref{thm:g30} has $72$ unit edges and forces $t\ge1$
(immediate from non-$5$-colorability; MaxSAT shows $t=1$ exactly).  A direct optimizer over the
$\Q(\sqrt3,\sqrt5)$ module universe --- iterated local search
minimizing the unit-edge count among $6$-chromatic
$\{1,\sqrt3\}$-graphs --- has already reached $m=70$ on $30$ vertices
(ratio $1/70$, pending exact re-verification).
%% TODO: exactly re-verify the m=70 graphs from the s3ratio runs.
The golden-ratio variant uses Proposition~\ref{prop:cap25}: a
$6$-chromatic $\{1,\tau\}$-graph forcing unit-edge monochromatic
density above $\tfrac25$ under $\tau$-proper $5$-colorings also proves
$\chi(\R^2)\ge6$; Parts' $31$-vertex graph \cite{Parts20a} measures
$1/57$ against that threshold.  The gap between $1/70$ and $1/3$ is
large, but Theorem~\ref{thm:win} makes it a single, well-posed finite
optimization --- raise $t$ (multi-fold forcing) and lower $m$ --- with
no invariant-mean caveats attached to the prize:
by Theorem~\ref{thm:impossible} the route through the caps at
$1/\sqrt3$ and $1/\tau$ is the only pairwise one available.

\section{An extended six-coloring continuum}\label{sec:continuum}

Following Mundinger, Pokutta, Spiegel and Zimmer~\cite{MPSZ}, let $X_6$ be the set of $d>0$ for which the plane has a coloring with six colors in which five color classes contain no two points at distance $1$ and the sixth contains no two points at distance $d$. They showed $X_6\supseteq[0.354,0.657]$ (to three decimals). Reaching $d=1$ would prove $\chi(\R^2)\le6$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:continuum}
$X_6\supseteq[0.354,\ 0.667504532081]$.
\end{theorem}

The new part is $[657/1000,\ 667504532081/10^{12}]$. It is covered by $59$ periodic tilings with rational vertex coordinates, each with eight polygonal tiles per period, from a $33$-parameter family whose mirror symmetry is broken. For each tiling an exact rational-arithmetic verifier checks that all tiles are simple polygons, that periodic interiors do not overlap, that the tile areas sum exactly to the area of the lattice cell, and that every same-color diameter and separation inequality holds strictly. The last check yields an open interval of distances $d$ that the sixth color avoids. A second script checks that these $59$ intervals overlap and cover the stated range. The numerical optimum of the family is $d=0.66750453208133\ldots$. At this point $23$ constraints are active with positive multipliers, and the reduced Hessian is negative definite, so it is a strict second-order local maximum of the tiling topology. Period doubling, $2\times2$, $3\times1$, $1\times3$ and $3\times2$ supercells, and all period-two relabelings of the five unit colors return to the same value. The certificates and verifiers are in the reproducibility bundle.

\section{Discussion and open problems}\label{sec:open}

\paragraph{Sparsity walls.}
On the negative side, the session derived quantitative lower bounds
(``sparsity walls'') showing that forcing results require substantial
graphs.  Over the Gwyn--Stavrianos framework \cite{GwynStavrianos20},
the master inequality
$\sum_{P} q_c(d_{ab}) + t\,e\,p_5(c)\ \ge\ t$
yields:
any $5$-color forcing unit-distance graph (a UDG forcing a
monochromatic pair at some distance $\dd'\ge\tfrac12$ in all proper
$5$-colorings) has at least $21$ vertices and $90$ edges; for
$\dd'=1$, at least $32$ vertices; at least $25$ vertices for families in
Parts' window, and $\approx25(r+1)$ under $r$-robustness; $t$-fold
forcing needs $n\ge17.9\,t^{3/4}$ vertices.  Moreover every
unit-distance graph on at most $15$ vertices is $5$-colorable (degeneracy
plus the exact maximum edge counts $u(n)$), and any $6$-chromatic
unit-distance certificate must have at least ${\sim}6993$ vertices (via
Parts' bound $\delta_6<1.43\times10^{-4}$).

\paragraph{Open problems.}

\begin{problem}
Is there a $25$-vertex $6$-chromatic two-distance graph?  The
$26$-vertex graph is vertex-critical and has survived extended attack;
no nontrivial lower bound on the minimum order is known for two-distance
graphs.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Finite $7$-chromatic witness]
Realize the incidence structure of
Proposition~\ref{prop:blueprint}/Theorem~\ref{thm:assembly} by an actual
finite $\{1,\dd\}$-point set, or answer Question~\ref{q:gatekeeper}.  A
positive answer to the gatekeeper question would put a
$\sim10^3$-vertex witness in reach; a $6$-color forced-same pair (two
points forced to share a color) would yield a $7$-chromatic graph
immediately by a spindle, at roughly $2|W|$ vertices.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Climbing to the win condition]
Push the ratio of Theorem~\ref{thm:win} from $1/70$ toward $\tfrac13$:
find $6$-chromatic $\{1,\sqrt3\}$-graphs with few unit edges and, more
importantly, with \emph{multi-fold} forcing ($t\ge2$) under
$\sqrt3$-proper $5$-colorings --- no construction with $t\ge2$ is
currently known.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:impossible}, pairwise mechanisms
can never beat $\tfrac12$ at the anchors $\sqrt3$ and $2$ themselves,
so progress must come either through the capped radii ($1/\sqrt3$,
$1/\tau$, and the star-polygon family), through non-pairwise machinery
at $\sqrt3$ or $2$, or through new inequalities feeding the
density-ledger LP of Section~\ref{sec:ledger}.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}
Determine $e_5$.  The greedy endpoint $2278$ is order-dependent
($2279$ on a second run); no matching lower bound technique for edge
counts of $5$-chromatic UDGs is known beyond the vertex-based walls
above.
\end{problem}
